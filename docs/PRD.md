# Product Requirements -- NIFTY Behavioral Options Engine, V1 (Paper)

## Objective

Receive genuine Half Trend Regression signals, evaluate current price behaviour
against validated historical evidence, reject weak setups, and paper-trade
eligible NIFTY options with controlled risk and a fully auditable record.

The edge must be demonstrated by out-of-sample evidence. It is not assumed from
the indicator's signals.

## In scope for V1

1. HTR BUY/SELL alerts from TradingView over an authenticated webhook.
2. Live Indian market data through a provider-neutral adapter layer: **Upstox**
   as the primary live feed, **FYERS** for historical research data (index
   ticks, OHLC candles, option quotes, instrument master). See
   `docs/ADR/0004-provider-neutral-market-data.md`.
3. Behavioural analysis of candle behaviour, market structure, momentum,
   volatility, pattern similarity and historical signal outcomes.
4. ACCEPT/REJECT of each HTR signal, with reason codes and evidence counts.
5. CE/PE selection with expiry, strike, spread, liquidity and lot-size checks.
6. Centralized risk limits and a kill switch.
7. Simulated entry, monitoring and exit from recorded market prices.
8. A complete, reproducible trading journal.
9. Chronological backtesting and forward paper validation against a raw-HTR
   baseline.

## Explicitly out of scope for V1

* **Any real order.** No `place_order`, no live-order route, no configuration
  toggle that could enable one.
* Independent directional signals generated by Python. The engine filters HTR
  signals; it does not produce its own. The Python HTR reference in
  `app/research/` is for parity testing and historical replay only, and is not
  imported by the live path -- see `docs/ADR/0005-python-htr-reference-is-research-only.md`.
* VWAP, RSI, MACD, Supertrend, moving-average crossover signals, previous-session
  directional bias, blind trend-following confirmation filters, unvalidated AI
  predictions, arbitrary indicator stacking.
* Using yesterday's direction to predict today's. Historical *observations* are
  in scope; yesterday-predicts-today is not.
* ML frameworks, Kubernetes, microservices, Redis, Kafka, Celery.

## Risk philosophy

Selective, small-risk trades. Trade frequency is not an objective. **Rejecting
every signal in a session is a valid outcome.** Pattern similarity never
guarantees a profitable trade, and a historical win rate is not a probability
for the next trade.

## Initial risk limits (design constraints, not guarantees)

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Maximum lots | 1 |
| Planned risk per trade | 1% of simulated capital |
| Daily loss limit | 2% |
| Maximum trade attempts | 2 per day |
| Concurrent positions | 1 |
| Overnight holding | Disabled |
| Expiry-day entries | Disabled initially |
| Real broker execution | Prohibited |

These are intended limits. They are not promises that realised losses cannot
exceed them: gaps, slippage and the full long-option premium are all at risk.

## Evidence rules

Any performance claim must state the exact data period, sample size, fees and
methodology. Simulated index-point profit is never reported as option profit.
A paper win rate above 50% is not grounds for recommending real-money
deployment. No backtest result is reported that was not produced by running the
code.
